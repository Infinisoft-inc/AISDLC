Why do humans stick to the identity they are given?
When a baby is born, as you said, they have no self-concept—no name, no identity, no narrative. But from early on, they are conditioned by their environment, especially caregivers. Their name, gender, cultural values, and even emotional reactions are taught or reinforced.

Name

Social roles

Cultural beliefs

Personality traits

Life story (autobiographical memory)

Over time, this becomes what we call the ego or self-concept—a kind of psychological "center" we identify with.

Why do people keep their identity?
Consistency and Coherence
Humans crave psychological consistency. A stable identity helps us feel coherent and in control. This is called "self-consistency theory" in psychology.

Social Validation
Identity is constantly reinforced by others. If people treat you as "Martin", you're more likely to keep being Martin. This is part of social identity theory.

Fear of Dissolution
Letting go of one's identity can feel like dying, psychologically speaking. People fear losing their sense of self—this is sometimes called ego death in spiritual or psychedelic contexts.

Memory-based Narrative

Self-concept permanence

Identity commitment
A concept in developmental psychology (James Marcia) where individuals commit to roles and beliefs even if those were externally imposed.

Cognitive closure
The desire for a firm answer and aversion to ambiguity. Once we "know who we are," we resist changing that.

Ego attachment (from Buddhism or depth psychology)
The clinging to the idea of a personal, separate self, often seen as the root of suffering.
